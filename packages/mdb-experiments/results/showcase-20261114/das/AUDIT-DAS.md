## Additional measurement: IIA tracks how much of the donor-recipient difference the subspace holds

From `cells.pt` (whose deltas match each record's `delta_norm` on 64/64), the median share of |v_donor - v|² inside each subspace:

| k | DAS | symtab | symcap | meandiff | random |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 0.22 | 0.29 | 0.27 | 0.20 | 0.06 |
| 16 | 0.39 | 0.50 | 0.49 | 0.38 | 0.12 |
| 32 | 0.56 | 0.73 | 0.67 | 0.56 | 0.26 |

In-sample: dasin16 0.67, dasin32 0.80, meanin32 0.85, symtab64 0.85. SAE per pair (rank about 4): 0.30.

The arms' order of IIA is the order of delta energy captured (symtab > symcap > DAS ≈ meandiff >> random), and flips appear only above about 0.6-0.7 of the energy. Energy is not the whole story: random64 holds about 0.5 and flips 1/64, while DAS32 holds 0.56 and flips 16. So direction matters at a given energy. Per dimension the SAE subspace is the most efficient handle measured (0.30 of the energy and 0.34 of the margin from about 4 dims, against 0.19 margin for symtab at k=4). It flips little only because it is small.

## Top corrections

1. Reword the vocabulary claim (report.md:73, 77, 79; tracker line 1173). The measured fact is that a readout span from the 38 training countries' tokens equals held-out DAS (16/64 at k=32). "Works only because the vocabulary includes the test countries" is not isolated from vocabulary size (69 vs 38 tokens). The data show captured energy rising steadily with the number of countries. Add the control: a 69-token span with no test country, or a per-pair leave-two-out span.
2. Soften "one direction per country, not a shared variable" (report.md:71, 79; tracker 1173) to "high-rank and only partly shared": a span fitted on 38 other countries holds 2x chance of a new country's delta and moves 0.60 of the margin.
3. dasin is more in-sample than stated (report.md:60). The reverse orientation of 60/64 test pairs is in its training set. Lean on meanin (no pairs) for the "rank is not the problem" claim.
4. symcap is test-informed at the metric level: it includes the readout rows of both scored answer tokens. Say so wherever symcap is cited as "training-free".
5. The SAE comparison is not rank-matched (report.md:77). At k ≤ 4 the SAE beats the readout span (4 vs 0 flips; 0.34 vs 0.19 of the margin moved). "Better insight" rests on the readout span scaling to k=32, which the per-pair SAE arm cannot do as built. The rank-matched SAE comparison (pushforward of all 58 census country features, or the top-k by activation, swept over k) was not run.
6. SAE PK chance is sqrt(4k)/128, not k/128 (report.md:37, 47).
7. Minor: `prereg_explore.json` says 42 training tokens (log: 38); `subspaces.json` provenance `driver_sha256` is the current das.py (`8e3c`), not the scored one (`88c1`); the test's `iia` assertion is vacuous.

## Plain answer to the owner's question

Does the symbol table give better insight than the SAE subspace, and can we map the subspace? Partly yes. The symbol table's country readout span, a fixed basis computed from weights and a declared token list, moves the country on 35/64 exp7 pairs at k=32 (31/38 at the 4-floor margin) and 43/64 at k=64. The exp7 SAE subspace moves 4/64. Those numbers are confirmed. Two caveats keep this from being a clean win. First, the SAE subspace is only about 4-dimensional, and at that rank it does better than the readout span (4 vs 0 flips), so the comparison is about scale rather than quality per dimension. Second, both handles are informed by the test countries: the readout span through their tokens, the SAE through their activations. Neither is a held-out map. On mapping: within the 28 test countries, about 16-32 of the cell's 128 dimensions carry the code. Class means with at most 27 directions reach 42/64 against 44 for the whole cell. So the subspace is mappable in the sense of being located and sized. It is not one compact "country variable": a subspace learned from 38 other countries, by DAS, class means or their token readouts alike, captures about twice chance of a new country's difference and flips only 14-16/64. More countries keep adding dimensions. Whether the readout span's lead comes from containing the test countries' tokens or simply from containing more countries is open. One cheap rerun with a larger, test-free token set would settle it.
