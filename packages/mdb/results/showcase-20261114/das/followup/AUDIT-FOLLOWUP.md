## Identity vs "the two directions most aligned with the delta" (Q3)

The question is whether these are the same thing. Mechanically, yes. The whole-cell swap is a rank-1 interchange along δ = v_donor − v_recipient. Any span that contains δ's direction reproduces the whole cell. The two countries' readout images are a weights-derived proxy for "the directions the donor-minus-recipient code moves along". lto removes exactly those two, and the data show they carry more than ordinary tokens do. 2 tokens are worth 13 flips, while 22 extra tokens are worth 2, and all 64 pairs lose delta energy.

The distinction matters for the reader, not for the number. "Test identity" here does not mean "test-set membership": the other 26 test countries add nothing (lto ≈ a69 ≈ aall in both flips and energy). It means "the span was told which two countries are being swapped". That makes the 13-flip increment a per-pair oracle term. It is the same kind of information the per-pair SAE set (saeC) uses, so it is not available to a held-out map of an unseen country. The report's wording ("the extra 13 flips come from containing the pair's own two token directions") is accurate. I would add one clause saying this is per-pair oracle content.

One control was not run: drop two random other test countries instead of the pair's two. lto vs a69/aall already makes it nearly redundant, because tokens other than the pair's two are interchangeable at this resolution.

## Top corrections

1. saeA is not test-free (report Q2 table and answer, prereg V5/labels). Its 58-feature pool was selected on items that include the 64 test items, and 54 of 58 features belong to test countries. Relabel it "weights-only ranking over a test-informed pool". The Q2 conclusion gets stronger, not weaker. An SAE pool chosen with test-country activations only ties a readout span with no test country (22 = 22; discordant 5 vs 5 at margin 1). At margin 4 it trails (10 vs 16). Against full69, the comparable test-informed span, it loses 22 vs 35 (1 vs 14 discordant, p = 1e-3). Replace "the SAE's 58 features are about as good as a generic 69-91 country readout span" with: "even with a test-informed feature pool, the SAE span only matches the test-free readout span, and trails it at the 4-floor margin."
2. Soften "above held-out DAS (16)" (Q1 nuance). 22 vs 16 at margin 1 is 8 vs 2 discordant pairs, p = 0.11. a69's 20 vs 16 gives p = 0.29. DAS is single-seed and untuned. The robust statement is "test-free readout span ≥ held-out DAS, and ≫ random (0/64)". The generic-structure conclusion stands on the random comparison.
3. Say that the 13-flip identity increment is per-pair oracle content: the span knows which two countries are swapped. It is not test-set membership, since the other 26 test countries add nothing.
4. V7 compares a per-pair SAE set (saeC) against a global readout span. The matched comparison would be the span of the pair's own two country readout images (k=2, per pair). It was not run, so "SAE is the better handle per dimension" is untested against an equally per-pair readout.
5. Minor. The decision rule's a69 = 20 sits exactly on the boundary, and aall = 22 is "mixed"; say so next to "V1 falsified". The Branches-M0 offset (≤ 0.375, 24/64 pairs) puts 5 full69_k32 verdicts within the offset of the threshold. That is the same convention as 11b and reproduces bitwise, but it is worth a footnote.

## Plain answer

The follow-up numbers are right. Every arm recomputes exactly from `out/`, the 11b reproductions are bitwise, and the vocabularies are clean (no test country, country token or answer token in a69/aall; lto drops exactly the pair's two countries). The main finding holds up well. Most of the readout span's lead over held-out DAS comes from containing the two countries being swapped: removing just those two tokens costs 13 of 35 flips, all from the same pairs (p ≈ 2e-4), while 22 extra generic tokens buy only 2. That is "identity" in the sense of a per-pair oracle, the same kind of information the per-pair SAE set uses. A span with no test country still reaches 20 to 22/64, far above random (0) and at least as good as held-out DAS, though the edge over DAS (22 vs 16) is not statistically clear on 64 pairs with one DAS seed. One label is wrong. The "test-free" SAE arm saeA draws its 58 features from a census built on the test items (54 of them are test-country features), so it is test-informed. This makes the SAE result worse, not better: even with that advantage, the SAE span only ties a readout span with no test country, and it loses 22 vs 35 to the comparable test-informed readout span. The prereg scorecard (V1, V2 falsified; V3, V4, V6, V7 held; V5's prediction failed without its falsifier firing) matches the sealed predictions.
